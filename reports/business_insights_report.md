# CloudDesk - Growth & Profitability Review (FY2024-FY2025)

> **CloudDesk is a fictional company and all data is synthetic**, generated by `scripts/generate_data.py`
> (seed 42). Every number in this report is computed by `analysis/business_insights.py` from the warehouse built in the
> same pipeline run; none is typed by hand. Use it to demonstrate method, not as industry benchmarks.

## Executive summary
- **Growth is strong but decelerating.** MRR grew from $148,633 to **$348,007** in 2025
  (+134%); ARR run-rate **$4,176,084**. Month-over-month growth slowed from
  10.2% (Jan) to 5.1% (Dec).
- **Retention is the main constraint.** Trailing-12-month NRR is **82%** and GRR **69%**;
  in 2025 churn and contraction removed $96,348 of MRR, equal to 39% of new MRR.
- **Activation is the biggest funnel lever.** 49% of accounts that create a project never invite a teammate;
  activated trials convert at 45% vs 12% for the rest.
- **28% of churn is involuntary (failed payments)** - the cheapest churn to fix.
- **Unit economics are healthy overall** (paid CAC $462); Content is the most efficient channel
  (8.8x LTV/CAC) and Partner the least (3.5x).
- **Outlook:** MRR is forecast to reach **$468,585** by June 2026 (80% interval $447,124-$490,046).

---

## 1. Which acquisition channels bring the most customers?
- **Finding:** Organic brings the most paying customers (1,700, 31% of all),
  followed by Paid search (1,365).
- **Metric:** paying customers from signups up to 17 Dec 2025 - Organic 1,700, Paid search 1,365, Referral 764, Paid social 735, Content 538, Partner 437.
- **Why it matters:** Organic has no media cost, so the business depends on SEO/brand for its largest source;
  paid search is the largest *paid* engine and the most exposed to CPC inflation.
- **Action:** protect organic (content/SEO investment, brand); diversify paid volume so no paid channel exceeds ~40% of paid customers.

## 2. Which channels generate the best conversion rates?
- **Finding:** Referral converts best signup-to-paid (23.3%), Paid social worst
  (18.5%). From the very top of the funnel, Referral has the best visit-to-paid rate (1.62%).
- **Metric:** signup -> paid: Referral 23.3%, Organic 21.4%, Partner 20.7%, Paid search 20.6%, Content 20.6%, Paid social 18.5%.
- **Why it matters:** Paid social also has the lowest onboarding rate (62% vs 72% median), so its traffic is lower-intent - paying for clicks that never activate.
- **Action:** launch a structured referral programme (credit for referrer and referee); tighten Paid social targeting
  and send its signups to a guided onboarding path.

## 3. Where are customers dropping out of the funnel?
- **Finding:** the largest in-product leak is **Project -> Activated** (49% lost). Before that, 28% of signups never finish onboarding.
- **Metric:** 928,479 visitors -> 26,538 signups (2.86%) -> 19,026 onboarded -> 13,307 created a project
  -> 6,793 activated -> 5,539 paying. 44% of payers never activated.
- **Why it matters:** activation is the strongest conversion predictor (45% vs 12%) and also lowers churn.
- **Action:** add an "invite your team" step into project creation, templates that require collaboration, and a day-3 nudge.
  *Scenario:* +5 points of project->activation would add ~218 paying customers over the period
  (~$15,849 MRR) at today's conversion rates.

## 4. Which customer cohorts retain the best?
- **Finding:** the best Month-6 cohort is Jan 2024 (91.2% still paying), the weakest
  Jun 2024 (78.3%). Across cohorts, Month-12 logo retention is **71.9%**
  and Month-12 revenue retention **85.9%** (expansion partly offsets lost logos).
- **Metric:** Month-12 logo retention by channel - Referral 78.5%, Partner 75.8%, Organic 72.6%, Paid search 71.7%, Content 67.0%, Paid social 65.6%.
- **Why it matters:** channel quality differs after the sale too: Referral customers retain
  13 points better than Paid social customers at Month 12.
- **Action:** judge channels on retained revenue, not first-month conversions; feed cohort retention into bidding (value-based bidding).

## 5. What are the main drivers of churn?
- **Finding:** churn is concentrated early (30% of churners leave within 2 months) and on the entry plan
  (customers who started on Starter churn 3.58% per month vs 1.64% for Business).
  The churn model's strongest signals are support tickets in the last 90 days, sessions per seat (engagement), recent failed payments and MRR / plan level.
- **Metric:** churn reasons - payment failure 28%, too expensive 20%, missing features 16%, switched competitor 15%, low usage 14%, business closed 6%.
  2025 churned MRR: $67,188. Model (out-of-time test): ROC-AUC 0.74,
  PR-AUC 0.32 vs a 8.2% base rate.
- **Why it matters:** 198 active customers are High risk today ($13,572 MRR); these are identifiable before they leave.
- **Action:** dunning upgrade for payment failures; CSM playbook triggered by support-ticket spikes and usage drops; first-90-days onboarding programme.

## 6. Which subscription plans contribute most to revenue?
- **Finding:** **Business** contributes 47% of MRR from 20% of customers.
- **Metric:** Dec-2025 MRR - Business $165,170 (47% of MRR, 830 customers), Pro $132,957 (38% of MRR, 1,683 customers), Starter $49,880 (14% of MRR, 1,720 customers).
- **Why it matters:** revenue is weighted to higher tiers, which also churn least - upgrades are worth more than extra Starter logos.
- **Action:** make the Starter -> Pro upgrade path explicit (seat limits, usage prompts); annual pricing on Pro/Business (the A/B test supports an annual-discount message).

## 7. Is revenue growth driven by new customers or existing customers?
- **Finding:** growth is **driven by new customers**. In 2025, new MRR was $245,112 while the existing base contributed a *net*
  -$45,738 (expansion $50,610, contraction -$29,160, churn -$67,188).
- **Metric:** net MRR added in 2025 = $199,374 = new MRR $245,112 minus $45,738 from the existing base
  (19% of new MRR lost by existing customers on net); NRR 82%.
- **Why it matters:** with NRR below 100%, the company must out-acquire its own churn every month; as the base grows, the same churn rate erases more new MRR.
- **Action:** set an NRR target (>= 95% next year) owned by Customer Success; launch expansion motions (seat-based add-ons, Business tier features).

## 8. Which channels have the best LTV/CAC?
- **Finding:** Content 8.8x (CAC $238, payback 3.6 months) is best;
  Partner 3.5x (CAC $776, payback 11.9 months) is weakest.
- **Metric:** Content 8.8x / CAC $238, Paid social 4.5x / CAC $394, Paid search 4.2x / CAC $487, Partner 3.5x / CAC $776.
  LTV = ARPA x 80% gross margin / monthly logo churn (assumptions in `docs/metric_definitions.md`).
- **Why it matters:** all paid channels clear the 3x rule of thumb, but marginal dollars should go where payback is shortest.
- **Action:** shift budget toward Content until its marginal CAC rises; renegotiate Partner terms or cap spend.
  *Scenario:* moving 20% of Partner's annual spend (~$33,908) to Content at its current CAC would buy
  ~143 customers instead of ~44 (assumes constant CAC - verify with incremental tests).

## 9. What is the projected revenue trajectory?
- **Finding:** MRR is projected at **$468,585** in June 2026 (+35% vs Dec 2025),
  ARR run-rate $5,623,020.
- **Metric:** model `saas_bridge` selected by rolling-origin backtest (MAPE 1.8%); 80% interval $447,124-$490,046.
- **Why it matters:** the forecast assumes current retention persists; each point of monthly churn removed compounds directly into this line.
- **Action:** use the 80% lower bound for hiring/spend commitments; track actuals monthly against the band.

## Limitations
- Synthetic data: relationships (e.g. activation -> conversion, tickets -> churn) were designed into the generator, so the analysis
  recovers them cleanly; real data will be noisier.
- No cost data beyond paid media (no sales salaries, tooling, COGS), so CAC and LTV are marketing-only and margin is assumed at 80%.
- Scenario impacts assume other rates stay constant; validate each action with an experiment before booking the benefit.
